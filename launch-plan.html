What happens between finished artwork and opening night

Roughly five weeks, most of it waiting on a printer. The work splits into four tracks that mostly run in parallel — but one of them blocks everything else, and it isn't the one that looks urgent.

Do this first

Build the two Google Forms before anything else, even though the digital side feels like it can wait until the cards exist.

Your QR codes have to point at a real form address, and they can't be generated until that address exists. The card backs need those QRs, and the standee needs the signup form's link. So both print jobs are stuck behind an afternoon of form-building. Everything else can slip; this can't.

Week 1

Stand up the digital side

An hour of setup, then an hour of trying to break it. Follow google-sheets/SETUP.md.

  • Create the spreadsheet and its three tabs Headers exactly as listed — the script finds columns by name.
  • Import the cards CSV 675 rows land in Cards, all showing unissued.
  • Build both Google Forms and connect them to the sheetblocks printing Signup form and activation form. Rename the response tabs to match the guide.
  • Copy the activation form's prefilled linkblocks printing The ⋮ menu → Get pre-filled link. This is the address your QRs encode.
  • Paste in the script and switch the trigger on New triggers are off by default. Nothing works until you flip it.
  • Write a privacy note and link it from the signup form You're collecting names and emails for marketing. Needs to exist before the form goes live, not after.
  • Test the whole journey on yourself, then try to break it Scan a code twice, try a rank you haven't earned, use an unregistered email. All three should refuse with a reason in the log.
Week 2

Marry the codes to the artwork

The step where a mistake gets expensive, so it ends with a physical check.

  • Regenerate the QR codes against the real form addressblocks printing The committed QRs point at a placeholder and will not work. Codes stay the same — only the images change, so nothing is wasted.
  • Scan a regenerated QR with your own phone It should open the form with the code already filled in. Thirty seconds, catches everything.
  • Send artwork and CSVs to the printer — one job per levelblocks printing Ranks 1–3 only for now: 250, 125 and 75 cards. Separate jobs make it impossible for the wrong codes to land on the wrong design.
  • Check the QR sits on a light panel, clear of the stamp grid Dark-on-light, 20mm minimum, quiet zone unbroken. Inverted QRs fail on a lot of phones.
  • Design the table standee around the signup form's link Runs in parallel with the card print. Cheaper and faster, so it can start later and still land first.
Week 3

Proof, then commit

Ask for a physical proof before the full run. Most printers will do one for very little.

  • Scan three cards from the proof, on a cheap phone, in dim light Bar lighting, not desk lighting. This is the real test.
  • Check the code printed on the card matches the level printed on it A misaligned batch is the one failure that reaches customers. Recoverable by editing the sheet, but far better caught here.
  • Approve the full run
  • Order the stamps and the d20 Two or three stamp designs so you can rotate them. Keep them behind the bar.
Weeks 3–4

While the printer has it

Dead time on the critical path. Spend it on the things that decide whether staff can actually run this.

  • Write the one-page staff crib sheet Card is full → hand over the next one → they scan it → they show you the screen → they roll. Plus: never hand out a card without seeing a full one first.
  • Decide the d20 prize table and tell staff what they can give away A roll nobody can honour is worse than no roll.
  • Decide exactly what earns a stamp Per visit, per drink, or per amount spent. This single choice sets how fast the whole ladder runs.
  • Print the standees
  • Agree who owns the Google account Not a personal account belonging to someone who might leave.
Week 5

Open

  • Walk the whole journey with a real card and a real phone Sign up, collect, scan, roll. Do it before a customer does.
  • Brief the staff, cards behind the bar, standees on tables
  • Put the red reorder marker three-quarters down the LVL 1 stack The sheet can't see your physical stock. This is what tells you to reprint.
  • Open the scheme
  • Diarise a review one month in Re-run the estimator with your real signup rate and real fill speed, then order ranks 4 and 5 with actual data.

The chain that actually sets your date

Build forms→ Get prefill link→ Regenerate QRs→ Merge into artwork→ Proof→ printer, 5–10 working days→ Open

Everything else — standees, stamps, staff briefing, prize table — runs alongside and can absorb delay without moving your launch. Only this chain moves the date, and the first two links are an afternoon's work that most people leave until last.

Three things still open

Not blockers today, but each one changes something downstream.

How many stamps fill a card — and is it already locked into the artwork?
If the stamp boxes are printed on the finished design, this is now fixed, and it's the single biggest driver of how fast members climb. Worth confirming the number matches what you intended before printing 450 of them.
What a stamp is earned for
Per visit is simplest and slowest. Per drink runs the ladder two or three times faster and would mean printing considerably more of ranks 4 and up than currently planned.
Whether level 1 needs scanning at all
Keeping it means one uniform rule and clean data. Dropping it saves variable-data printing on your largest batch. Either is fine — but it must be settled before the level 1 cards are printed, because it decides whether they carry codes.
